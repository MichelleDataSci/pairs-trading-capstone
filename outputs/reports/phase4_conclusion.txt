
PHASE 4 -- TESTING MEAN REVERSION ON UNSEEN DATA (JAN-JUL 2026)
================================================================

Both pairs selected in Phase 3 were tested on genuinely unseen 2026 data.

METHODOLOGY (applied identically to each pair)
-----------------------------------------------
  Training window : 2018-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (full 8-year history)
  Normalisation   : 2025 residuals only (250 trading days)
                    mu and sigma are fixed from 2025 and not updated in 2026.
                    This is stricter than Phase 3b's rolling 30-day z-score --
                    any drift in the spread level or variance in 2026 shows up
                    as persistent z-score deviation.
  Test period     : 2026-01-02 to 2026-07-31 (145 trading days)
  Z-score         : Z_t = (spread_t - mu_2025) / sigma_2025
  Signals         : stateful entry/exit -- enter long at Z < -2, enter short
                    at Z > +2, exit at Z = 0 (same rules as Phase 3b backtest)

PAIR 1: AMZN / META  [Primary -- passes both EG and Johansen at 5%]
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  OLS direction   : AMZN ~ META  (AMZN as dependent variable)
  Hedge ratio     : 0.5977
  Intercept       : 1.5323
  R-squared       : 0.787

  2025 normalisation:
    mu    : -0.0359
    sigma :  0.0607

  Test period results (Jan-Jul 2026):
    Trading days  : 145
    Z-score mean  : +2.003  (benchmark = 0 for perfect mean reversion)
    Z-score std   :  1.584
    Z-score range : -1.15 to +5.43

  Trading signals:
    Long  entries (Z < -2.0) :  0
    Short entries (Z >  2.0) :  2
    Days in market           : 99  (68.3%)

  The spread drifted persistently ABOVE the 2025 equilibrium from the start
  of 2026.  Both short signals were triggered; the first eventually exited
  at Z = 0, the second remained open at 31 July.  No long signals fired.

PAIR 2: MSFT / AAPL  [Secondary -- Johansen trace 10% only, supervisor-approved]
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  OLS direction   : MSFT ~ AAPL  (MSFT as dependent variable)
  Hedge ratio     : 0.8710
  Intercept       : 1.3022
  R-squared       : 0.957

  2025 normalisation:
    mu    : +0.0870
    sigma :  0.1186

  Test period results (Jan-Jul 2026):
    Trading days  : 145
    Z-score mean  : -2.421  (benchmark = 0 for perfect mean reversion)
    Z-score std   :  0.933
    Z-score range : -4.26 to -0.40

  Trading signals:
    Long  entries (Z < -2.0) :  1
    Short entries (Z >  2.0) :  0
    Days in market           : 124  (85.5%)

  The spread drifted persistently BELOW the 2025 equilibrium throughout
  the test period.  One long signal fired (Z < -2) and remained open at
  31 July without reaching the Z = 0 exit.  No short signals fired.

COMPARATIVE ASSESSMENT
-----------------------
  Both pairs show WEAK mean reversion over Jan-Jul 2026.

  AMZN/META : persistent positive drift (+2.0 sigma above 2025 equilibrium)
  MSFT/AAPL : persistent negative drift (-2.4 sigma below 2025 equilibrium)

  The drift directions are opposite, which rules out a single macro factor
  pushing both spreads in one direction.  Instead, each pair's constituent
  stocks appear to have experienced idiosyncratic relative re-pricing in
  early 2026 that was not consistent with the 2018-2025 cointegrating
  relationship.

  This is a valid empirical result and should not be adjusted to appear more
  favourable.  The cointegration evidence from 2018-2025 does not guarantee
  short-window mean reversion in unseen data -- especially for AMZN/META,
  whose EG p-value (0.014) already sits close to the 5% threshold, and for
  MSFT/AAPL, which was included as a borderline secondary candidate.

IMPLICATIONS FOR PHASE 5
--------------------------
  The Phase 4 results directly motivate the machine learning phase.
  A fixed cointegration model cannot distinguish periods of genuine mean
  reversion from regime shifts.  A predictive model trained on spread
  features (z-score level, z-score momentum, VIX, hedge ratio stability)
  could potentially identify conditions where mean reversion is more likely,
  allowing the strategy to hold off during persistent drift episodes like
  those observed in Jan-Jul 2026.
